Experiment 24: High Eval Bias (1.5) -- Constant Bias with OOD Evaluation
=========================================================================

Question:
  Does evaluating at higher bias (1.5 vs 0.7) reveal stronger alpha/phi
  correlation with obstacle proximity, even with the same trained policy?

Hypothesis:
  Under more extreme persistent bias (1.5, well above training max of 1.0),
  the policy should be forced to rely more heavily on phi near obstacles,
  revealing whether the correlation exists but was masked at lower eval bias.

Note:
  Eval bias 1.5 is OUT OF DISTRIBUTION -- training max was 1.0. This tests
  generalization of the learned alpha+phi policy to unseen disturbance levels.

Setup:
  - Copy of experiment 22 (constant bias) with ONE change: eval bias = 1.5
  - Training: disturbance_range = (0.0, 1.0) -- identical to exp 22
  - Eval: EVAL_BIAS_MAGNITUDE = 1.5 (was 0.7 in exp 22)
  - Bias type: constant per episode (random direction, fixed magnitude)
  - Methods compared:
      1. Dynamic alpha+phi (ISSf-CBF): agent controls [kx, ky, alpha, phi]
      2. Dynamic alpha-only (standard CBF): agent controls [kx, ky, alpha]
  - 8 hand-picked scenarios (worst-case upward bias) + 100 random scenarios
  - 2M training timesteps per model, PPO, 8 parallel envs
