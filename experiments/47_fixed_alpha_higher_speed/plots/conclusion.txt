Experiment 47: Fixed Alpha=1.0 + Higher Speed + Two-Level Sensor Error
===================================================================

Based on: Exp 43 (fixed alpha) + Exp 45 (higher speed + sensor error)

Motivation:
-----------
Exp 43 showed the strongest phi proximity-adaptive behavior by fixing
alpha=1.0. This experiment applies fixed alpha to the improved setup:
6 m/s, larger environment, systematic sensor error.

Setup:
------
- Action: [kx, ky, phi] — alpha FIXED at 1.0
- Max speed: 6 m/s, target at x=150, 800 max steps
- Sensor error: systematic bias U(-0.6, 0.4) + jitter U(-0.2, 0.2)
- Dynamics bias: U(0.3, 1.0), obstacle radius: U(3.0, 7.0)
- Collision penalty: -100
- 10M steps, 16 envs

Results:
--------
- ~8/10 reached — much better than exp 43 (5/10)
- Speed: hovers ~5-6 m/s. Aggregate speed vs distance DOES show speed
  dropping from ~6 down to ~1-2 m/s at close distances — the speed
  contrast we wanted. But the robot rarely gets that close.
- Phi: flat at ~0.01. A few scattered points to ~0.05-0.10 but no
  proximity-adaptive behavior. Phi did NOT learn here.

Conclusion:
-----------
Surprising result: exp 43 (3 m/s, small env) showed phi learning 0.0-0.7,
but this experiment (6 m/s, large env) shows phi flat at 0.01 — despite
both having fixed alpha=1.0.

The difference is NOT the speed — it's the environment geometry relative
to the speed. At 6 m/s in a large environment, the robot has enough room
and speed to steer around all obstacles without ever getting close. Phi
is never needed, so it never learns.

At 3 m/s in a smaller environment, the robot was forced into close
encounters, making phi necessary for survival.

Key insight: phi learning depends on whether the robot faces UNAVOIDABLE
close encounters with obstacles, not on the speed itself. The training
environment needs to create situations where close approach is inevitable.

Future direction: instead of changing speed, consider tighter obstacle
placement (narrow gaps, corridors) that force close encounters regardless
of speed.
