Experiment 45: Higher Speed + Systematic Sensor Error
======================================================

Based on: Experiment 44 (systematic sensor error model)

Motivation:
-----------
In exp 40, alpha's proximity behavior was hard to see because max speed
3 m/s was too low — CBF at different alpha values all allow near-max speed.
Raising to 6 m/s with larger environment should make speed contrast visible:
fast in open areas, slow near obstacles.

Setup:
------
- Action: [kx, ky, alpha, phi]
- Max speed: 6 m/s (action space [-6, 6])
- Environment: target at x=150, obstacle bands [20-50, 60-100, 110-140]
- Sensor error: systematic bias U(-0.6, 0.4) + jitter U(-0.2, 0.2)
- Dynamics bias: U(0.3, 1.0), obstacle radius: U(3.0, 7.0)
- Collision penalty: -100, progress*50
- 10M steps, 16 envs

Results:
--------
- ~9-10/10 reached — excellent navigation
- Speed: hovers at ~6 m/s (max speed) the entire time with barely any
  dips. The speed contrast we hoped for is NOT showing. The robot goes
  full speed everywhere and steers around obstacles at distance.
- Alpha: drops near obstacles, most visibly on the last obstacle. But
  alpha dropping doesn't translate to speed reduction because the robot
  maintains enough clearance that the CBF constraint never bites on speed.
- Phi: more activity than exp 40 (range ~0.01-0.7), but still no clean
  proximity-adaptive trend. Alpha remains the dominant safety knob.

Conclusion:
-----------
The robot learned to AVOID rather than SLOW DOWN. Given progress reward,
the optimal strategy is max speed + steer around at safe distance. This
is why speed contrast doesn't appear — the robot never enters the regime
where the CBF constraint actually limits speed.

Alpha drops near obstacles but the robot is far enough away that even
the tighter constraint doesn't reduce speed. Phi showed more activity
than exp 40, which is promising.

Key insight for future experiments:
-----------------------------------
To see real speed contrast, we need scenarios where the robot CAN'T avoid
getting close to obstacles:
  - Narrow gaps between obstacles forcing the robot to slow down
  - Tighter obstacle placement blocking the direct path
  - Or: fixed alpha (exp 47-50) where the robot can't adapt alpha to
    maintain speed, forcing phi to be the only lever

The navigation-vs-visibility trade-off: good navigation means the robot
stays far from obstacles, which means alpha/phi behavior near obstacles
is never stressed. We need to create conditions where close approach is
unavoidable.
