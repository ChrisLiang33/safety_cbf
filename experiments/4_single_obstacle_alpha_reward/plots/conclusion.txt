Experiment 4: Single Obstacle + Alpha Reward Bonus
====================================================

Question:
Does the alpha reward bonus (reward += 0.3 * alpha) help in the simpler 1-obstacle
setting? This is the single-obstacle counterpart to exp 3, designed to isolate the
effect of the alpha bonus from the added complexity of multiple obstacles.

Setup:
- Dynamic agent: [alpha, kx, ky] action space (alpha in [0.1, 5.0], kx/ky in [-2, 2])
- Baselines: fixed alpha = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0] with learned [kx, ky] steering
- 1 obstacle (radius=1.0), same layout as exp 1
- Single-integrator dynamics, 900K PPO timesteps, 8 parallel envs
- Reward: progress (50x), time penalty (-0.5), lateral penalty, + 0.3*alpha bonus

Observations:
- Trajectories (10 hand-picked scenarios):
  - Dynamic alpha paths are smooth and efficient, similar to exp 1.
  - Alpha coloring shows warmer tones (higher alpha) in open space compared to exp 1,
    consistent with the alpha bonus pushing the agent toward higher alpha values.
  - In some scenarios the dynamic trajectory cuts closer to the obstacle than exp 1,
    reflecting the more aggressive alpha profile.

- Alpha Adaptation:
  - Clear alpha-vs-distance inverse relationship preserved across all scenarios.
  - Open-space alpha values appear slightly higher than exp 1, consistent with
    the bonus incentivizing high alpha when safe.
  - Near-obstacle alpha dips are still present but may be slightly shallower.

- Aggregate Metrics (100 random scenarios):
  - Target Reached: alpha=0.1 77%, alpha=0.5/1.0 100%, alpha=5.0 100%, Dynamic 100%.
  - Safety Violations: 0% across all methods.
  - Safety Margin: alpha=0.1 ~2.92m, Dynamic ~1.28m, alpha=5.0 ~1.09m.
    Dynamic is slightly closer to alpha=1.0 (~1.40m) territory.
  - Path Efficiency: Dynamic ~0.94, near-optimal, similar to exp 1.

Conclusion:
The alpha bonus in the 1-obstacle setting produces nearly identical results to the
base experiment (exp 1). Dynamic safety margin is ~1.28m (exp 1 was ~1.27m), success
rate is 100%, and path efficiency is ~0.94. The alpha bonus has essentially no
measurable effect in this simpler environment — the progress reward already provides
enough incentive for the agent to use high alpha in open space.

Combined with exp 3's results, the conclusion is that the alpha reward bonus is
unnecessary reward shaping. The natural task reward (reach target quickly, avoid
collisions) is sufficient to drive appropriate alpha behavior. Adding explicit alpha
incentives provides at most marginal benefit and risks distorting the learned policy.

Future Steps:
- Abandon the alpha bonus approach — the natural reward is sufficient.
- Try CBF intervention penalty instead (exp 5) — a fundamentally different reward
  shaping idea that targets the QP correction rather than the alpha value itself.
