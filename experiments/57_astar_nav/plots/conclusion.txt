Experiment 57: A* Navigation → Train Alpha/Phi
================================================
NOT YET RUN

Motivation:
-----------
Same problem as Exp 56: joint training starves alpha/phi of signal.
This experiment replaces the learned navigator entirely with A* path
planning — a classical algorithm, no learning, no gradients.

Mirrors SHIELD paper architecture:
  SHIELD: A* → safety filter → RL locomotion → robot
  Ours:   A* → learned adaptive CBF (alpha, phi) → robot

Why A* may be better than frozen learned nav:
  - A* gives optimal shortest paths — WILL route through tight spaces
  - Learned navigator may still give obstacles wide berth (habit from training)
  - A* is deterministic — no noise from a learned policy
  - Clean experimental isolation: if alpha/phi don't learn, it's not navigation's fault

A* Implementation:
------------------
- Discretize env into 1m occupancy grid
- Inflate obstacles by estimated_radius + 1m margin
- Standard A* with 8-connected neighbors
- Proportional waypoint following (K=4.0, threshold=3.0m)
- Replan every 50 steps

Setup:
------
- Action: [alpha, phi] only — kx, ky from A*
- SHIELD SDF CBF (Eq 19-22), single constraint
- Collision -250
- Sensor error: systematic bias U(-0.6, 0.4) + jitter U(-0.2, 0.2)
- Dynamics bias: U(0.3, 1.0), obstacle radius: U(3.0, 7.0)
- Max speed: 6 m/s, target at x=150, 800 max steps
- 10M steps, 16 envs

What we hope to find:
---------------------
1. Strong alpha/phi training signal because A* routes through tight spaces
2. Alpha/phi adapt to proximity consistently
3. Compare to Exp 56: if A* produces better results, learned nav still
   avoids obstacles too aggressively. If similar, the sequential structure
   is the key insight.

Potential concerns:
-------------------
- A* replanning every 50 steps on a 170x44 grid adds CPU overhead per step.
  May reduce FPS significantly. If too slow, increase grid resolution or
  replan less frequently.
- The inflation margin (est_radius + 1m) means A* won't route extremely
  close to obstacles. May need tuning.
