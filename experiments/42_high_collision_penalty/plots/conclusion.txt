Experiment 42: High Collision Penalty
=======================================

Motivation:
-----------
Phi was stuck at 0.01 in exp 40. Does a stronger collision penalty (-500
instead of -100) push the policy to use phi as a safety tool?

Setup:
------
- Action: [kx, ky, alpha, phi], phi range [0.01, 10.0]
- Collision penalty: -500 (was -100)
- Everything else same as exp 40: bias U(0.3,1.0), error U(-1.0,0.0),
  obstacle radius U(3.0,7.0), progress*50, 10M steps, 16 envs

What we hoped to find:
----------------------
Stronger collision penalty creates more pressure to use phi for safety,
resulting in phi increasing near obstacles.

Results:
--------
- ~7-8/10 reached — comparable to exp 40
- Alpha: similar range and behavior to exp 40
- Phi: much more activity than exp 40! Phi ranges from 0.01 up to ~1.0
  in the aggregate policy map, with visible spread at closer distances.
  Not a clean proximity-adaptive trend — more noisy/scattered — but
  clearly the policy is using phi more than in exp 40 where it was flat.
- Speed: similar to exp 40

Conclusion:
-----------
Higher collision penalty did create more phi activity — the policy is no
longer ignoring phi entirely. However, the behavior is not a clean
"phi increases as distance decreases" pattern. It's more of a general
"be cautious sometimes" response. Still, this is the most phi movement
we've seen outside of exp 38 (extreme conditions).

Takeaway: higher collision penalty is a useful component to carry forward
into future experiments. The penalty of -500 may be too aggressive;
a moderate range like -200 to -300 combined with other improvements
(systematic sensor error, higher speed) could produce cleaner results.
