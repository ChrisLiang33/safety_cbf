Experiment 13: Disturbance Weave (3 Obstacles + Noise)
======================================================

Question:
Does the dynamic alpha agent remain robust when additive disturbance noise corrupts
the robot's dynamics? The standard CBF assumes perfect dynamics (x_dot = u), but
real systems have model uncertainty. Here we add uniform noise to the position update:
x += safe_u * dt + noise * dt, where noise ~ U(-scale, scale). The disturbance scale
is domain-randomized per episode (0.0 to 0.1 m/s), so the agent must learn a policy
that works across varying noise levels.

Setup:
- Dynamic agent: [kx, ky, alpha] action space (kx/ky in [-3, 3], alpha in [0.1, 5.0])
- Baselines: fixed alpha = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0] with learned [kx, ky] steering
- 3 obstacles (radius=5.0 each), same slalom layout as exp 9
- Disturbance: uniform noise, scale domain-randomized in [0.0, 0.1] per episode
- Evaluation at fixed noise=0.05
- 100m map, single-integrator dynamics, 2M PPO timesteps, 8 parallel envs

Observations:
- Combined Scenarios (8 hand-picked):
  - Dynamic alpha successfully weaves through all scenarios despite the noise.
  - Trajectories show slightly more jagged paths compared to exp 9 due to the
    disturbance, but the overall weaving structure is preserved.
  - Robot speed profiles are similar to exp 9 — three dips at each obstacle.
  - Alpha shows the same three-spike pattern, robust to the noise perturbation.
  - Alpha-vs-distance relationship is preserved despite the stochastic dynamics.

- Alpha Adaptation Details:
  - The alpha spikes are similar to exp 9 in magnitude and timing.
  - The agent doesn't appear to be significantly more conservative despite the
    noise — it trusts the CBF to handle small perturbations.
  - The domain randomization during training (noise in [0, 0.1]) appears to have
    produced a robust policy that doesn't overreact to disturbance.

- Aggregate Metrics (100 random scenarios, noise=0.05):
  - Target Reached: alpha=0.1 ~21%, alpha=0.5/1.0/5.0 100%, Dynamic 100%.
  - Safety Violations: 0% across all methods. The CBF still prevents collisions
    even with disturbance at this scale.
  - Safety Margin: Dynamic ~0.93m (tighter than exp 9's ~1.43m). The noise
    occasionally pushes the robot closer to obstacles.
  - Path Efficiency: Dynamic ~0.98, same as without noise.
  - Episode Length: Dynamic ~367 steps, essentially identical to exp 9's ~369.
  - Average Speed: Dynamic ~2.01 m/s, matching exp 9.

Conclusion:
The dynamic alpha policy is robust to additive disturbance at the tested noise levels.
Performance metrics are nearly identical to exp 9 (no noise): same success rate
(100%), same speed (~2.0 m/s), same path efficiency (~0.98). The only notable
difference is a tighter safety margin (0.93m vs 1.43m), which is expected since the
noise can push the robot closer to obstacle boundaries unpredictably.

The 0% collision rate despite noise confirms that the standard CBF constraint
(Lgh @ u >= -alpha * h(x)) is robust to small disturbances in practice, even though
it doesn't explicitly account for them. The noise scale (0.05 m/s * 0.1s = 0.005m
per step) is small relative to the obstacle radii (5m), so the CBF has sufficient
margin to absorb it.

The domain randomization strategy (training with noise in [0, 0.1]) was effective —
the agent learned a general policy rather than overfitting to a specific noise level.
