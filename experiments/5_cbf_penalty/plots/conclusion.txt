Experiment 5: CBF Intervention Penalty (2 Obstacles)
=====================================================

Question:
Does penalizing the CBF intervention (reward -= 1.0 * ||safe_u - k_nom||) lead the
agent to preemptively avoid triggering the safety filter? The idea is that the QP
correction ||safe_u - k_nom|| measures how much the CBF had to modify the agent's
desired control. By penalizing this, the agent should learn to either steer around
obstacles more cleanly or lower alpha near obstacles so the constraint is already
satisfied before the QP runs.

Setup:
- Dynamic agent: [alpha, kx, ky] action space (alpha in [0.1, 5.0], kx/ky in [-2, 2])
- Baselines: fixed alpha = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0] with learned [kx, ky] steering
- 2 obstacles (radius=1.0 each), same layout as exp 2
- Single-integrator dynamics, 900K PPO timesteps, 8 parallel envs
- Reward: progress (50x), time penalty (-0.5), lateral penalty, - 1.0*||safe_u - k_nom||

Observations:
- Trajectories (9 hand-picked scenarios):
  - Dynamic alpha trajectories are smoother and give slightly wider berth to obstacles
    compared to exp 2. The agent seems to anticipate the CBF boundary and steer
    around it rather than relying on the QP to correct its path.
  - In "Straight Gauntlet" and similar tight scenarios, the dynamic agent takes a
    cleaner line that avoids large QP corrections.
  - Fixed baselines are identical to exp 2 (they don't have the penalty).

- Alpha Adaptation:
  - Alpha still shows the characteristic inverse-distance pattern.
  - The alpha dips near obstacles may be slightly deeper than exp 2, consistent with
    the agent lowering alpha to reduce the severity of QP corrections near boundaries.

- Aggregate Metrics (100 random scenarios):
  - Target Reached: alpha=0.1 69%, alpha=0.5/1.0/5.0 100%, Dynamic 99%.
  - Safety Violations: 0% across all methods.
  - Safety Margin: alpha=0.1 ~2.48m, Dynamic ~0.96m, alpha=5.0 ~0.83m.
  - Path Efficiency: Dynamic ~0.99, the best across all methods.

Conclusion:
The CBF penalty produces the most path-efficient dynamic agent so far (~0.99,
essentially straight-line optimal). The agent learns to preemptively steer around
obstacles so the QP barely needs to intervene. The safety margin (~0.96m) is
slightly larger than exp 2's (~0.90m), suggesting the agent gives a bit more
clearance to avoid triggering the CBF correction rather than cutting close and
relying on the filter.

The 99% success rate (vs 100% in exp 2) is a minor regression — one scenario
likely timed out. The CBF penalty creates a slight incentive tension: the agent
is penalized for being near obstacles (where the QP corrects more), which could
occasionally cause overly cautious detours that lead to timeouts.

Overall, the CBF penalty is a promising reward shaping approach — it produces
cleaner, more anticipatory behavior than the raw progress reward alone. It teaches
the agent to work *with* the CBF rather than *against* it.

Future Steps:

- Scale to larger environments with 3 obstacles (exp 9) and test the CBF penalty
  approach there (exp 12).
