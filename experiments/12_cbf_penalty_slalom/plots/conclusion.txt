Experiment 12: CBF Penalty Slalom (3 Obstacles)
================================================

Question:
Does the CBF intervention penalty (reward -= 5.0 * ||safe_u - k_nom||) improve the
agent's behavior in the 3-obstacle slalom layout? Experiment 5 showed this penalty
produced the most path-efficient agent on 2 obstacles. Here we test whether the same
approach scales to the 100m, 3-obstacle weave from exp 9. The hypothesis is that the
penalty will teach the agent to preemptively steer around obstacles more cleanly and
adjust alpha to minimize QP corrections.

Setup:
- Dynamic agent: [kx, ky, alpha] action space (kx/ky in [-3, 3], alpha in [0.1, 5.0])
- Baselines: fixed alpha = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0] with learned [kx, ky] steering
- 3 obstacles (radius=5.0 each), same slalom layout as exp 9
- CBF penalty weight: 5.0 (stronger than exp 5's 1.0, scaled for larger map)
- 100m map, single-integrator dynamics, 2M PPO timesteps, 8 parallel envs
- Reward: progress (50x), time penalty (-1.0), - 5.0*||safe_u - k_nom||

Observations:
- Combined Scenarios (8 hand-picked):
  - Dynamic alpha successfully weaves through all scenarios.
  - Trajectories appear to give slightly wider berth to obstacles compared to exp 9,
    consistent with the agent learning to avoid triggering CBF corrections.
  - Robot speed dips are slightly shallower near obstacles — the agent's preemptive
    steering reduces the magnitude of CBF intervention, keeping speed higher.
  - Alpha shows three per-obstacle spikes similar to exp 9, but the spikes are
    slightly lower, suggesting the agent relies more on steering and less on alpha
    to avoid intervention.
  - CBF intervention (col 2) shows lower peak magnitudes than exp 9, confirming the
    penalty works — the agent learned to minimize the QP correction.

- Alpha Adaptation Details:
  - Similar three-spike pattern to exp 9, but with lower peak alpha values.
  - The agent's strategy shifted: rather than raising alpha aggressively near obstacles
    (which allows the QP to pass larger k_nom through), it steers more carefully and
    uses moderate alpha, resulting in less intervention overall.

- Aggregate Metrics (100 random scenarios):
  - Target Reached: alpha=0.1 21%, alpha=0.5/1.0/5.0 100%, Dynamic 100%.
  - Safety Violations: 0% across all methods.
  - Safety Margin: Dynamic ~1.84m (larger than exp 9's ~1.43m), alpha=5.0 ~0.56m.
    The CBF penalty pushes the agent to maintain more clearance.
  - Path Efficiency: Dynamic ~0.98, same as exp 9.
  - Episode Length: Dynamic ~380 steps, slightly slower than exp 9's ~369 steps.
  - Average Speed: Dynamic ~1.98 m/s, similar to exp 9.

Conclusion:
The CBF penalty successfully modifies the agent's behavior in the 3-obstacle slalom.
The most notable effect is the increased safety margin (1.84m vs 1.43m in exp 9) —
the agent gives wider berth to obstacles to avoid triggering the QP filter. Path
efficiency remains near-optimal, and success rate stays at 100%.

The tradeoff is subtle: the CBF penalty agent is slightly slower than exp 9 (~380 vs
~369 steps) because it takes wider paths around obstacles. But the reduced CBF
intervention means the agent's intended control (k_nom) is executed more faithfully
by the QP, producing smoother, more predictable motion.

Comparing to exp 5 (CBF penalty on 2 obstacles): the approach scales well. The
penalty produces cleaner, more anticipatory behavior regardless of obstacle count.
However, the improvement over the base approach (exp 9) is incremental rather than
transformative — the unpenalized agent already learns good behavior, and the penalty
mainly shifts the safety-speed tradeoff toward slightly more conservative but
smoother trajectories.
