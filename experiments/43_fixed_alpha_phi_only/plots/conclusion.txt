Experiment 43: Fixed Alpha + Phi Only
=======================================

Motivation:
-----------
In all previous experiments (40-42), the policy could use alpha instead of
phi for safety, so it never bothered learning phi. By fixing alpha at 1.0
and removing it from the action space, phi becomes the ONLY safety knob.
If phi can't learn under these conditions, it can't learn at all.

Setup:
------
- Action: [kx, ky, phi] — alpha FIXED at 1.0, NOT learned
- Phi range: [0.01, 10.0]
- Dynamics bias: U(0.3, 1.0), radius error: U(-1.0, 0.0)
- Obstacle radius: U(3.0, 7.0), 10M steps, 16 envs
- Collision penalty: -100, progress*50

What we hoped to find:
----------------------
With alpha removed as a competing safety knob, phi is forced to learn
proximity-adaptive behavior or the robot fails.

Results:
--------
- 5/10 reached — worst navigation of exp 40-43, which makes sense since
  alpha is locked and can't adapt
- Phi: MOST INTERESTING RESULT OF ALL EXPERIMENTS. The aggregate policy
  map shows a visible general trend: phi tends to be higher at closer
  distances (~0.4-0.6 near obstacles) and lower far away (~0.0-0.3).
  Not a perfect curve, but clearly more structure than any other experiment.
  Multiple scenarios show this pattern consistently.
- Speed: robot slows near obstacles — since alpha is fixed, only phi
  tightens the CBF constraint, and speed drops confirm phi is active.

Conclusion:
-----------
This is our strongest evidence that phi CAN learn proximity-adaptive
behavior when forced to. Removing alpha as a competing safety knob is
the key ingredient. Navigation suffers (5/10 vs 8/10 in exp 40), but
the phi signal is real and consistent.

Takeaway: the fixed-alpha approach should be carried forward into newer
experiment setups (systematic sensor error, higher speed, higher collision
penalty) to see if phi learning improves further with better conditions.
