Experiment 11: Tight Slalom (Close Obstacle Encounters)
========================================================

Question:
What happens when the slalom obstacles are placed much closer to the path centerline?
Experiment 9 placed obstacles at y in [5,8] (surface 0-3m from y=0). Here we move
them to y in [2,4] — with radius=5, the surface reaches y in [-3,-1], meaning the
robot passes within 1-3m of the obstacle boundary. This forces very close encounters
where steering alone can't avoid heavy CBF intervention. Does dynamic alpha still
help when the robot must pass extremely close to obstacles?

Setup:
- Dynamic agent: [kx, ky, alpha] action space (kx/ky in [-3, 3], alpha in [0.1, 5.0])
- Baselines: fixed alpha = [0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0] with learned [kx, ky] steering
- 3 obstacles (radius=5.0 each) in TIGHT slalom:
  - Obs 1: x in [20,35], y in [+2, +4] (surface reaches y=-3 to y=-1)
  - Obs 2: x in [45,60], y in [-4, -2] (surface reaches y=+1 to y=+3)
  - Obs 3: x in [65,80], y in [+2, +4]
- Target at x=100, y in [-2,2], radius in [1.5,3.0]
- 100m map, single-integrator dynamics, 2M PPO timesteps, 8 parallel envs

Observations:
- Combined Scenarios (8 hand-picked):
  - Dynamic alpha navigates all 8 tight slalom scenarios successfully.
  - Trajectories show tighter weaving paths than exp 9 due to closer obstacles.
  - Robot speed shows deeper dips at each obstacle — the CBF intervenes more heavily
    when the robot passes within 1-3m of the surface.
  - Alpha shows higher spikes near obstacles compared to exp 9, reflecting the
    agent's need to raise alpha more aggressively to maintain speed through the
    very tight passages.
  - CBF intervention (col 2) shows pronounced peaks at each obstacle, with the
    dynamic agent showing spikier but shorter interventions than fixed baselines.

- Alpha Adaptation Details:
  - Alpha spikes are taller (~4-5) and more abrupt than exp 9, matching the closer
    obstacle encounters.
  - The agent learned that tight passages require aggressive alpha to prevent the
    CBF from stalling the robot entirely.
  - Between obstacles, alpha drops rapidly to moderate levels (~1-2).

- Aggregate Metrics (100 random scenarios):
  - Target Reached: alpha=0.1 83%, alpha=0.5/1.0/5.0 100%, Dynamic 100%.
  - Safety Violations: 0% across all methods.
  - Safety Margin: alpha=0.1 ~6.1m (avoids obstacles entirely by going way around),
    alpha=0.5 ~3.0m, Dynamic ~1.50m, alpha=5.0 ~0.56m.
  - Path Efficiency: Dynamic ~1.01, alpha=0.5 ~1.75 (large detours), alpha=5.0 ~0.98.
  - Episode Length: Dynamic ~375 steps, alpha=5.0 ~313 steps, alpha=0.1 ~498 steps.
  - Average Speed: Dynamic ~2.0 m/s, alpha=5.0 ~2.4 m/s.

Conclusion:
The tight slalom amplifies the differences between methods. alpha=0.1 improves to
83% success (vs 21% in exp 9) because the tight layout actually creates clearer
paths that the overcautious agent can eventually find, but at massive inefficiency
(1.75 path efficiency = 75% longer than optimal). Dynamic alpha maintains 100% success
with near-optimal paths (~1.01 efficiency) and reasonable margins (~1.50m).

The tight layout is where dynamic alpha's per-obstacle adaptation matters most. The
agent must raise alpha aggressively at each close encounter (to prevent stalling) but
can't leave it permanently high (which would reduce margins elsewhere). The three
distinct alpha spikes are taller and sharper than exp 9, showing the agent adapted
its strategy to the tighter geometry.

alpha=0.5 shows the widest margin (~3.0m) because it detours significantly around
obstacles. Dynamic alpha passes much closer (~1.50m) but at nearly optimal path
efficiency — a much better tradeoff.
